# Adversarial review of the tableau Casimir bound

The coordinate proof of Theorem 10 survives the review. Its decisive step is an **identity for the deficit** in a column estimate. If a box is swapped in turn with the members of an alternating column of height $s$, the sum $Y$ of those swaps satisfies
$$
  \|f\|^2-\operatorname{Re}\langle f,Yf\rangle
     =\frac{\|(I-Y)f\|^2}{s+1}.
$$
There is one upper charge per source box, however tall the target. Symmetry allows the shorter of two columns to supply all the source boxes. Exactly that many labels occur in both columns. These facts account for the coefficient, including its sign and its factor of two.

Locations below give the printed PDF page and the line numbers of the supplied `paper.tex`. Lean locations refer to the supplied archive under `targets/alpoge/lean/src`. The local proof files are in `Development/WeilBound/RepresentationTheoryGL/CasimirAlgebra/KugaCoordinates/CasimirBounds/`. The verdict concerns the mathematical argument for the stated tableau bound.

11. **OK — Exterior transfer and normalization.** P. 3, lines 188–196, pp. 8–9, lines 539–567, and p. 14, lines 1005–1009, 1023–1027. Equip wedges of increasing basis vectors with norm one. In the ordinary tensor product of the two exterior powers, the transfer $T=\sum_i A_i\otimes A_i^\dagger$ lowers the first degree and raises the second. Expanding its square gives
    $$
    T^\dagger T=\sum_{ij}A_i^\dagger A_j\otimes(\delta_{ij}I-A_j^\dagger A_i)
    = h_c I-X_{cc'}.
    $$
    Here $A_i^\dagger A_j$ represents the Lie algebra action of $E_{ij}$, the **sum over slots** within that wedge. Contraction between the two sums gives all cross swaps once in this order. Reversing source and target gives $h_{c'}I-X_{cc'}$. Thus the ordered deficit for source $c$ is both $\|Tf\|^2$ and the sum of the individual squared defects divided by $h_{c'}+1$. The latter follows by summing finding 5, with no requirement that the individual defects be orthogonal. The Lean analogues are the slot contraction at `Expand.lean:32–55` and the coordinate deficit at `ReSwapSumLe.lean:56–83`. The exterior maps themselves are the authors' mathematical comparison.

12. **OK — Highest weights, scalar direction and the content argument.** Pp. 4–5, lines 271–328, p. 8, lines 498–513, and pp. 13–14, lines 927–945, 971–981, 1010–1022. On a highest-weight vector, commuting positive roots to the right in $\sum E_{ij}E_{ji}$ gives $\sum_j\mu_j^2+\sum_{j<k}(\mu_j-\mu_k)=\Gamma_n(\mu)$. The scalar matrix acts by $d$ and its trace-dual is $I/n$, giving the correction $d^2/n$ for $n>0$. With $j=n-i$, the linear coefficient $n+1-2j$ becomes $2i+1-n$. A tensor weight is a sum of wedge weights. Each wedge supplies at most $\min(k,h_c)$ entries among the first $k$, proving $D_k\ge0$, with $D_n=0$ by total degree. Summation by parts gives $\sum_{k<n}D_k(v_k-v_{k+1})$, with each difference at least two. Restriction to $U(n)$ gives a unitary decomposition, and complexification gives the same Lie algebra constituents. This bounds arbitrary sums of vectors in those constituents.

    For the permutation proof, let $H=\prod_c S_{h_c}$ and let $\alpha$ be the partition of column heights. By restriction/induction reciprocity a shape $\mu$ supporting the column signs must occur in $\operatorname{Ind}_H^{S_d}\operatorname{sgn}|_H$. Twisting by sign gives occurrence of $\mu'$ in the Young permutation module of type $\alpha$. Thus $\mu'$ dominates $\alpha$, and $\mu$ is dominated by $\alpha'=\lambda$. Twice the content sum is $\sum_j\mu_j(\mu_j+1-2j)$, which becomes $\Gamma_n(\mu)$ on adding $nd$. This argument concerns the shapes meeting the alternating subspace. Individual cross swaps can move vectors out of that subspace. The central transposition sum preserves it. The coordinate route expands the operator at `Expand.lean:66–92` and applies the tableau count at `CasBoundHolds.lean:19–32`.
