# Cold read of v2

The paper gives me enough to explain the bound and its coordinate proof. The overview on p. 2 is unusually effective: it states the decisive estimate and explains the shorter-column choice before the formal machinery. The main difficulty is holding that picture in view through the label-based proof in §§3.3–3.5. My comments concern the exposition, not an independent verification of the Lean or citations.

## Explanation to a colleague

1. Expand the Casimir: it is n times the number of selected boxes, plus the swaps of all ordered pairs of distinct selected boxes.
2. A swap inside a column acts by −1, so a height-h column contributes nh − h(h−1).
3. For a box p outside an alternating set B, let Y be the sum of the swaps of p with B.
4. Antisymmetrizing over B together with p gives (f−Yf)/(|B|+1).
5. Because antisymmetrization is an orthogonal projector, the form of Y is at most ||f||².
6. For two columns, sum this estimate over the boxes of the shorter column, using the longer column as B.
7. Thus their cross-swaps contribute at most the shorter height times ||f||², for each ordering.
8. Add the diagonal, within-column and cross-column contributions to obtain the height coefficient.
9. The tableau conditions make each column's labels a terminal interval; two columns therefore share exactly the shorter height many labels.
10. Counting these same contributions by label gives the coefficient in the formal theorem.

What I had to reconstruct: how the proof's equal-label comparison is the shorter-column argument in disguise, and how the per-box formula in Proposition 9 combines the three contributions. The ingredients are all present, but the bridge is dispersed across pp. 4, 9–12 and sometimes relegated to remarks.

## Where the thread frays

- **p. 2, paragraph beginning “Derivation of the bound”**: Theorem A and Theorem B(a) are invoked before either is stated. I can infer the strategy, but not yet see why labels can be introduced without changing the tensor problem. Say explicitly that labels are auxiliary and can be chosen independently of f and K, then point forward.
- **p. 2, “Cartan component … largest highest weight”**: “Cartan component” and the ordering implicit in “largest” arrive before explanation. Section 5 supplies the meaning, but a short parenthesis defining it as the constituent generated by the product of highest-weight vectors would help. “Rank” for n on p. 3 can also suggest semisimple rank rather than the dimension of the defining space.
- **p. 3, notation immediately before the tableau condition**: Q, C, γ, b, S, d, Sc, hc, CS and mi arrive at once. The distinction between fixed labels b and varying tensor coordinates x is crucial but only appears on p. 4. Flag it here, and bring the diagram alongside the definitions.
- **p. 5, Theorem B(b), “name the two columns”**: the tie choice and Yp depend on the pair, but the notation suppresses that dependence. I had to reread the formula to know which Yp was meant. Write Yp^{c,c′}, or state explicitly that it changes with the outer summand.
- **p. 7, Jucys–Murphy paragraph before Proposition 3**: “content,” “addable boxes,” and “seminormal” are used without definitions. This interrupts the route from the projector to the elementary proof; it is background, not needed to understand the bound. Move it to a later remark or define content and illustrate the two boxes.
- **pp. 8–9, Propositions 4–5**: the counts are clear individually, but their purpose is delayed until Proposition 9. Before Proposition 4, show the prospective per-box contributions n, −2(n−1−b(p)), and mb(p)−1, identifying the last as a cross-column bound rather than an entrywise bound.
- **pp. 9–10, Propositions 6–8**: the general kernel R, arbitrary signed N, indicator function and match argument obscure the already-explained shorter-column geometry. The height translation appears only in a remark after Proposition 7. Put the nested-terminal-interval picture before its proof and explain why the general signed version is being retained: it matches the formal lemma. The warning on p. 10 that comparison is not entrywise is essential and should precede the displayed comparison.
- **p. 11, proof of Proposition 9, “Adding the diagonal term”**: the coefficient suddenly appears per box. Explicitly display n − 2(n−1−i) + (mi−1) = mi + 2i + 1 − n and then group the mi boxes of label i. State that this is a global accounting, not a separate estimate for each box, at this point rather than only in the following remark.
- **p. 12, proof of Theorem B(a)**: it refers back to the proof of Proposition 9 to establish a purely combinatorial equality. A standalone two-line calculation using Σi mi(mi−1) = 2Σ{c,c′} min(hc,hc′) would make the bridge accessible without retracing the formal proof.
- **pp. 13–15, highest-weight and transfer proofs**: useful alternative perspectives, but the switch to reversed basis order and λj = mn−j demands a mental rotation of Figure 1. Annotate the figure with j = n−i. In §5.2 say at the outset that transfer is an alternative certificate of the same pair deficit, not another prerequisite for the coordinate proof.
- **p. 2, “The archive needs the constant exactly”**: the motivation is suggestive but “coefficient representation” and “twist” are not yet explained. A plain sentence stating the comparison's two sides and what the twist accomplishes would help; otherwise defer the claim to §6.

## Fixes ranked by reader benefit

1. Put a one-page proof map before §3: expansion → within-column signs → one-box estimate → shorter-column estimate → label count, with the three numerical contributions to the coefficient displayed together.
2. Introduce Proposition 7 with the nested-interval diagram and the warning that equal-label matching bounds a whole column-pair sum, not individual swaps; keep the general lemma afterward for fidelity to Lean.
3. Display the per-box arithmetic in Proposition 9 and give the independent overlap count in Theorem B(a), linking the height and label formulas in both directions.
4. Reduce the initial notation load and flag fixed labels versus varying coordinates before either is used; clarify the pair dependence of Yp.
5. Move the Jucys–Murphy aside out of the main proof path and add brief definitions or forward pointers for Cartan component, content and the application terminology.
