# Referee report on the tableau Casimir bound, version 2

**Recommendation to the editor — minor revision.** The paper succeeds at its principal task. A reader can explain why an arbitrarily tall alternating column costs at most one unit in the estimate, and can then locate the calculation that implements that idea in Lean. The two main theorems are correct. The most worthwhile editorial change is to bring the projector proof and its equality criterion together. They give a much shorter route through the paper for its intended reader.

## 1. Summary

Let a finite set of slots index copies of $\mathbb C^n$. Each slot belongs to a column and has a fixed label between $0$ and $n-1$. Labels are distinct within a column and are closed upward there. Select a union $S$ of whole columns and write $m_i$ for the number of selected boxes of label $i$. On the selected factors take the diagonal Lie algebra action and its trace-form Casimir $K=\sum_{ij}G_{ij}G_{ji}$. For a tensor $f$ alternating within every column, the formal theorem bounds its Casimir quadratic form by $\sum_i m_i(m_i+2i+1-n)\|f\|^2$. The labels enter the scalar alone. The operator acts on the tensor coordinates.

The paper recovers the geometric meaning of this expression. Each column of height $h$ contributes $h(n+1-h)$, and each unordered pair contributes at most twice its smaller height, in units of $\|f\|^2$. This is the top Casimir eigenvalue on the product of the selected exterior powers. The formal proof produces the estimate directly from swaps. The exposition also identifies the entire loss in the estimate as a sum of squares.

In the archive a selected set is the fiber of a complex embedding. The coefficient representation embeds isometrically into the column-alternating tensors. Subtracting a scalar twist $t$ from each diagonal generator shifts the fiber Casimir by $-2t|S|+nt^2$. The corrected upper bound is precisely the automorphic Casimir scalar required by the smallness condition. With the other hypotheses of the Kuga argument, the differential vanishes on cochains, and a nonzero cochain gives cohomology. This supplies an archimedean representation-theoretic input on the Weil-bound side of the archive. It explains why even a small positive error in this particular estimate would matter.

## 2. Significance and novelty

A graduate reader of representation theory may already know the extremal eigenvalue and still learn something here. Knowing which irreducible summand has the largest Casimir does little to explain the proof one encounters in the source, where the main inputs are a swap inequality and a comparison of two finite sums. Conversely, a reader of the source could verify every one of those sums without recognizing the Cartan component. Equations (2) and (3) close that gap. This is worth publishing as exposition, especially for mathematicians trying to read formal proofs across specialties.

The credits are substantially fair. The paper credits Alpöge and Claude for the archive, identifies the automated origin of the exposition, and ascribes no priority to the bound. Lévy gives the tensor-slot contraction, Yang gives the scalar for the precise trace-form normalization, and Gross explicitly counts row and column overlaps. The Jucys–Murphy interpretation explains the two possible contents when a box is added to a column. The references to skew Howe duality put the exterior transfer in context without requiring that the cited quantum-group paper state Proposition 11 verbatim. The two positivity arguments also deserve their separate references and treatments. The elementary positivity in (2) comes from an orthogonal projection. Kuga's positivity concerns a differential on a cochain complex. [Lévy, proof of Proposition 2.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0703690) · [Yang, Example 5.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.12225) · [Gross, Appendix A.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/hep-th/9212149)

**Recommended.** The closest short precedent for §5.1 is already in the bibliography in a different connection. In [33], Proposition 27.1 and §§27.2–27.3, Etingof uses Casimir separation to identify the summand generated by the product of highest vectors, specializes to exterior powers, and explains how the determinant factor enters for $GL_n$. Cite it beside Proposition 10. This is more informative about the classical status of the whole argument than separate references for its ingredients. [Etingof, *Representations of $GL_n$, I*](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-755-lie-groups-and-lie-algebras-ii-spring-2024/mit18_755_s24_lec01.pdf)

**Optional.** A natural additional reference for readers pursuing the equality conditions is K. Akin, D. A. Buchsbaum and J. Weyman, “Schur functors and Schur complexes,” *Advances in Mathematics* **44** (1982), 207–278, especially §II on Schur functors. Contraction followed by insertion is familiar from the exterior-power presentation and its exchange relations. Cite this as context for the maps, leaving the proof of the metric identity in §5.2 in place. [Original article](https://jerzyweyman.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/Publ1-Schur-Functors-and-Schur-Complexes.pdf)

## 3. Correctness

I compared Theorem A and Propositions 1–9 with the supplied Lean sources and checked the deductions in Theorem B and §5. My formal-source check was a reading of declarations and proofs. The compilation and axiom results on p. 3 remain attributed to the runs reported there. Following the local imports gives the stated 975 lines in ten files, including 466 in the five proof files. A source search finds the single direct use of the named theorem at `ClozelCohomologicalOfThree.lean:11`.

- **Pp. 5–8, Theorem A and Propositions 1–3.** The hypotheses in `StepFormula.lean:88–94` include alternation on all of $Q$. The sum in `cas_expand'` is over ordered distinct slots. In `re_swapSum_le'`, the three-slot relation puts the internal transposition next to $f$, giving $Y^2f=sf-(s-1)Yf$. For $g=(I-Y)f$ this gives $Yg=-sg$. Self-adjointness now gives $\|g\|^2=(s+1)\langle f,g\rangle$, including the divisor in (2). The declaration exports the inequality. The identity is visible inside its proof.
- **Pp. 8–12, Propositions 4–9 and Theorem B.** The label sets of two active columns are nested terminal intervals. Thus the source of the one-box estimate can always be chosen to have a unique match for each of its boxes. Symmetry supplies the other order. This is precisely the two-case argument of `CountCross.lean:28–57`, and it also explains why the formal kernel lemma works for signed $N$. No multiplication of an inequality by $N$ of unknown sign is needed. In (3), summation over the shorter column gives the defect for one order. Doubling it gives Theorem B(b), and positivity of every weight gives (c), including the vacuous single-column case.
- **Pp. 13–16, classical checks.** Reversing the basis gives $\lambda_j=m_{n-j}$. In Proposition 10 the partial-sum difference has zero final term, and successive coefficients in the summation by parts differ by at least two. This gives strict separation for every other dominant highest weight. In Proposition 11 the anticommutator produces one number operator on the source wedge, hence $h_c I$, and the remaining term is the slot contraction $X_{cc'}$. Both the normalization and the sign agree with (3).
- **Pp. 16–17, application.** In `CoefGlueHolds.lean:120–150` the inequality is applied to $Jv$; intertwining, $J^*J=I$, the shift, and the arithmetic identity are separate inputs. The text correctly explains the need for a bound on every coefficient vector. The distinction on p. 2 between ordinary RH in the joint verification statement and the wider contents of the archive is also accurate. [Lean FRO and Mathlib Initiative, joint statement](https://leanprover-community.github.io/blog/posts/riemann-hypothesis/)

The corrections below concern a citation detail, the precision of the downstream paraphrase, and the scope of the review record. I found no gap requiring a change to the inequality, its equality criterion, or the application.

## 4. Presentation as an exposition

The opening road map works. By the end of p. 2 a reader can predict the coefficient without knowing a highest-weight formula. Figure 1 makes the reversed row indexing intelligible. The running example earns its space particularly on p. 10, where a positive swap expectation between boxes of *unequal* label rules out a tempting entrywise interpretation. The counterexample on p. 13 likewise explains exactly which use of the original labels fails under partial selection.

**Recommended.** Give a reader who knows elementary multilinear algebra a continuous proof of (1). After the coset calculation on p. 7, sum (2) over the shorter column, double for the two orders, and add the internal swaps. This takes one paragraph and reaches Theorem B, including equality. Keep the coordinate calculation immediately available as the answer to “how does Lean prove the projector estimate?” Consolidate Propositions 4–9 as the translation back to the label count, retaining their declaration numbers in the concordance. Currently six numbered steps intervene before the reader sees what summing the projector identity achieves.

**Recommended.** Keep the opening disclosure, the formal/supplementary distinction in §1.3, and the source concordance. Shorten the abstract by removing the process parenthesis and the directions to a paragraph following (1). A single sentence can say which form Lean proves and which the article derives. Move the model roster, the itemized revision history, and the separate `typePrimesN` grading exercise to an online supplement. Section 9 candidly explains what its scores measure, but an eight-point comparison on another theorem cannot tell the editor whether this paper's reader understands (2). A brief production note and a dated reference to the checks would give the mathematical article a cleaner ending.

## 5. Specific comments

1. **Recommended — pp. 5 and 13–14, §§2.2 and 5.1.** Write $K=K_S\otimes I_{V'}$. In the equality statement the admissible space on the spectator slots is itself column-alternating. If that space is denoted by $A_{\mathrm{out}}$, the full admissible equality space is $L_\lambda\otimes A_{\mathrm{out}}$. The present wording is correct for an already admissible $f$, but can be misread as a description of all equality vectors without the standing hypothesis.
2. **Recommended — pp. 6–8 and 12, running example and (3).** At $n=2$ the target column of height two is full. In fact *every* admissible tensor in that example attains equality, since antisymmetrizing three slots gives zero. Say so. For a strict case with the same diagram, take $n=3$ and a unit totally alternating three-tensor. Then $Yf=-2f$, the single-order defect is $9/3=3$, and the Casimir value is $9-6=3$ against a ceiling of $9$. This one calculation exhibits the loss measured by Theorem B.
3. **Recommended — p. 7, §3.1, final paragraph.** Refine the description of [18]. Lévy uses the unordered convention for the transposition Laplacian, but the *last display of his proof* gives the ordered sum over distinct slots, exactly as in Proposition 2 with the opposite Laplacian sign. Pointing there lets the reader check both the sign and the factor two directly. [Lévy, p. 6 of the preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0703690#page=6)
4. **Optional — p. 15, Proposition 11.** Give the target bidegree of $T$ as $(h_c-1,h_{c'}+1)$ and interpret exterior degree $n+1$ as zero. This also explains the equality in the running example. In §5.3 on p. 16 specify partitions of $d$ with at most $n$ rows and define the content of a box as its column index minus its row index.
5. **Recommended — p. 16, §6.1, and p. 25, references.** The text hyperlinks “Grobner–Raghuram, §6” but supplies no corresponding bibliography entry. Add H. Grobner and A. Raghuram, *On some arithmetic properties of automorphic forms of $GL_m$ over a division algebra*, arXiv:1102.1872, Theorem 6.3 and the first sentence of its proof. That passage explicitly invokes Clozel's Lemme 3.14 for the split cuspidal case. [Authors’ paper](https://homepage.univie.ac.at/harald.grobner/papers/GLn%28D%29.pdf)
6. **Recommended — p. 17, §6.2, final substantive paragraph.** Make the implication concerning Hermitian tests explicit. The Young step supplies `altPair tl η ≠ 0`. Then `om_ne` in `CochainHolds.lean:49–105` proves the contracted cochain is nonzero: if all tests vanished, they would recover zero for every coordinate of the paired alternation. The phrase “tests against Hermitian matrices detect that the paired alternation is nonzero” obscures which nonvanishing is given and which is proved. For the intervening passage from the nonzero cochain to a scalar tensor, add `CoefGlueHolds.lean:63–84` to Table 3.
7. **Essential — p. 23, §10, paragraph beginning “The checks on record”.** Date and identify the completed v2 checks underlying the assertion that reviewers checked the new statements during review of v2, or limit the sentence to the drafting-stage checks documented for this submission. Section 8.2 inventories reviews of v1, while §10 makes a claim about checks of v2 itself. A reader needs to be able to tell which revision a report examined. This is a matter of provenance, with no bearing on the algebra in Theorem B.

## 6. Recommendation and venue

**Minor revision.** The proofs and the account of why the constant matters can stand. The essential change is local. The suggested cuts would make the article more inviting while preserving the full concordance for readers working alongside Lean. My preferred journal venue for a shorter version is *Elemente der Mathematik*. The calculation with a signed average of $s+1$ cosets gives a reader with linear algebra a route into a specialized question, a good fit for that journal's expository remit. The complete 25-page version has a natural second use as a companion to the Lean archive. Its case for a journal rests on helping a mathematician reconstruct the argument, independently of the size or prominence of the surrounding proof.
