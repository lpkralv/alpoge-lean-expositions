# Referee report on the exposition of `casBound_holds`

**Recommendation: major revision.** The coordinate proof is sound, and there is a short expository article here. Its subject is how column antisymmetry controls a sum of transpositions strongly enough to recover the sharp Casimir bound. The manuscript identifies this idea on page 2. A reader must then work through an automorphic construction, a literature survey, a second road map and a glossary before seeing the calculation. For a paper whose contribution is to make a formal proof understandable, that order warrants revision.

## 1. Summary

Take a tensor power of \(\mathbb C^n\) with finite slot set \(Q\). Group its slots into columns. The fixed labels in a column of height \(h\) run once each through \(n-h,\ldots,n-1\). The tensor \(f\) changes sign under any transposition within a column. Select a union \(S\) of whole columns and let \(m_i\) count its slots of label \(i\). With the diagonal action of \(\mathfrak{gl}_n\) on the selected slots and the trace pairing, Theorem 10 asserts
\[
 \operatorname{Re}\langle f,Kf\rangle\le
 \sum_{i=0}^{n-1}m_i(m_i+2i+1-n)\,\|f\|^2 .
\]
The other slots may be entangled with the selected ones. The operator acts on them by the identity. The statement allows rank zero, when the existence of the label map forces the slot set to be empty.

In the archive, the selected slots form the fiber of an embedding of a totally complex number field. Coefficient gluing applies the estimate to the isometric image \(Jv\) of an arbitrary coefficient vector. After the diagonal twist, the ceiling is exactly the automorphic Casimir scalar for that embedding. Thus each coefficient-minus-automorphic difference has nonpositive quadratic form. A separate Kuga identity and positivity argument force the cochain differential to vanish. A nonzero cochain then gives cohomology. This is where the lemma enters the cohomologicality theorem for regular algebraic cuspidal representations used in the Weil-bound development. Even a small excess in the ceiling would compromise that sign argument.

## 2. Significance and novelty

A representation theorist can recognize the coefficient immediately upon reversing the rows. With \(\lambda_j=m_{n-j}\), it is the trace-form Casimir eigenvalue on the Cartan component of \(\bigotimes_c\bigwedge^{h_c}\mathbb C^n\). All constituent weights are dominated by \(\lambda\). The literary contribution is to explain how the Lean proof reaches this extremal eigenvalue using matrix units, permutation signs and counting alone. That is worth publishing for readers of formal mathematics and for graduate mathematicians who know tensor products but have yet to study highest weights. The survey of nine write-ups gives a reason to document this particular proof. Its scope supports no claim of priority for the inequality, and the paper generally respects that limit.

**Recommended (pp. 3–5, 8).** Keep three precise historical connections. Lévy [15, Prop. 2.2] identifies the tensor Casimir with a scalar and a sum of swaps. His compact Laplacian uses the opposite sign, and each unordered transposition occurs twice in the present \(K\). The enlarged-column antisymmetrizer in [27–30] explains the factor \(1/(k+1)\). Gross [33, Appendix A.1] already counts intersections of rows to convert the Casimir formula between rows and columns. None of these attributions requires crediting those authors with the full Lean argument. Likewise [16, Lemma 4.2.3] describes the exterior transfer generators. The authors of this exposition supply the calculation identifying a transfer square with the pair deficit. Put that distinction at the calculation, then remove the repeated citations for taking the smaller of two heights. [Lévy](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0703690), [Cautis, Kamnitzer and Morrison](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1210.6437), [Gross](https://arxiv.org/pdf/hep-th/9212149)

**Recommended (pp. 2–4).** Two missing references would help more than additional sources for the Casimir scalar. A. Borel and N. Wallach, *Continuous Cohomology, Discrete Subgroups, and Representations of Reductive Groups*, second edition, AMS, 2000, chapter II, develops the scalar product, Laplacian and Casimir on relative Lie algebra cochains. L. Clozel, “Motifs et formes automorphes: applications du principe de fonctorialité,” in *Automorphic Forms, Shimura Varieties, and L-functions*, vol. I, Perspectives in Mathematics 10, 1990, pp. 77–159, gives the automorphic context for regular algebraic representations and cohomology at infinity. Cite these for the destination of the formal argument. Also credit Alpöge's stated collaboration with Claude on the underlying archive when first introducing it. Page 1 currently gives a much clearer account of the models' roles in the exposition than in the source. [Borel and Wallach](https://doi.org/10.1090/surv/067), [Clozel's article in the proceedings](https://www.jmilne.org/math/Books/AA1988a.pdf), [Alpöge's account](https://alpo.ge/remark.pdf)

## 3. Correctness

I compared the stated hypotheses with `KTypeData.lean:51–52` and `StepFormula.lean:88–94`, and followed the proof in the supplied source. This was a source review without a fresh Lean build. I found no mathematical error in Theorem 10 or Propositions 1–9.

* **Pages 7–9, Propositions 1–3.** Contracting the two matrix units gives \(nI\) on a repeated slot and one swap on distinct slots, as in `Expand.lean:32–55,66–92`. For \(p\notin B\), write \(Y=\sum_{q\in B}P_{pq}\) and \(k=|B|\). In an off-diagonal term the identity \(P_{pq}P_{pq'}=P_{pq'}P_{qq'}\) puts an internal swap directly against \(f\). Hence \(Y^2f=kf-(k-1)Yf\). Self-adjointness gives \(\|(I-Y)f\|^2=(k+1)\langle f,(I-Y)f\rangle\), including at \(k=0\). This is the argument of `ReSwapSumLe.lean:29–83`. It needs no estimate on the operator norm of \(Y\).
* **Pages 9–13, Propositions 4–9 and §2.7.** For distinct columns, sum the one-box inequality from the shorter into the taller. Interchanging source and target preserves the double sum. The shorter height is exactly the number of shared labels because the label intervals have the same upper endpoint. Thus the cross terms contribute at most \(\sum_i m_i(m_i-1)\|f\|^2\). The within-column terms contribute exactly \(-2\sum_i m_i(n-1-i)\|f\|^2\). Adding \(n|S|\|f\|^2\) gives the printed coefficient, with both orders accounted for. This agrees with \(\mathtt{count\_up},\mathtt{count\_eqb},\mathtt{count\_cross},\mathtt{cas\_count}\) and the final specialization in `CasBoundHolds.lean:21–32`. The extension to a signed real scale in Proposition 7 is valid. One can deliberately choose the orientation whose height counts matches even when multiplying by a negative scale reverses numerical order.
* **Pages 8 and 13, companion arguments.** The contraction and wedge anticommutation relation gives \(T^*T=h_c I-X_{cc'}\) in the indicated direction. The summation by parts in the highest-weight proof has zero boundary term because both weights have degree \(|S|\). Its coefficients \(v_j-v_{j+1}\) are positive. Both arguments work. The requests below about the wedge action and orthogonality concern explanation.
* **Pages 2–3, application.** The sole direct use of the named theorem is at `ClozelCohomologicalOfThree.lean:11`. The actual instantiation through the hypothesis `hCB` occurs in `CoefGlueHolds.lean:120–150`. The sign of the twist correction there is \(-2s|S|+as^2\) at rank \(a\), as printed. The page 1 count of 975 lines also agrees with the ten local source files in the transitive import closure. It measures the size of those files, including definitions and other declarations.

**Essential factual correction (p. 1, §1, first paragraph).** The sentence about certification follows one naming both RH and Dirichlet GRH. Its wording extends the certification to “the archive's statement and proof.” The joint statement identifies the formal target as Mathlib's ordinary `RiemannHypothesis`. Name that target and cite the certifying parties directly. Give Alpöge's announcement separately for the scope of his archive. The local absence of `sorry`, `admit` or `axiom` concerns those ten source files. A claim about the logical axioms used transitively would require a different check. [Joint statement of the Lean FRO and the Mathlib Initiative](https://leanprover-community.github.io/blog/posts/riemann-hypothesis/)

## 4. Presentation as an exposition

**Essential (pp. 2–13).** Put the height formula beside the theorem. A column of height \(h\) earns \(nh\) from the diagonal and loses \(h(h-1)\) from alternation. An unordered pair of columns adds at most twice the shorter height. These statements already give the entire bound in a form one can remember. Then explain the one genuinely surprising step. On tensors alternating in the target column, \((I-Y)/(k+1)\) is antisymmetrization over that column and the new box. This explains both the square and its divisor before the three-slot multiplication proves the identity in the manner of Lean. Finish by counting shared labels. The road map on page 2 has the right ingredients. What costs the reader is that §§2.1, 2.4 and 2.5 keep anticipating a calculation whose proof only starts on page 7.

**Recommended (pp. 3, 9–14).** Use the two-column example in Proposition 8 throughout. At rank two, heights two and one give labels \(\{0,1\},\{1\}\). Both swap expectations in the example are \(1/2\), including the one joining unequal labels. The upper bound counts one common label, yet it bounds the sum of both swaps. The diagonal, internal and cross terms are then \(6,-2,2\), giving \(6\), exactly the coefficient for \((m_0,m_1)=(1,2)\). This example teaches the distinction between a slot's permanent label and the varying coordinate of a tensor. Keep the partial-column counterexample on pp. 9–10. Move the general signed-kernel statements, proof-declaration concordance and detailed supplier chain to an appendix. The conventions for the inner product and permutations fit beside their first use. The glossary entries for the real part and the integers, and most of §2.8, can go.

**Essential (pp. 1–2, 10, 13, 15–16).** Keep a short, visible disclosure of who generated the text and who assumes responsibility for publication. Replace “checked by hand” at first use and throughout with “supplementary argument outside Lean, checked by an AI model,” or equivalent. The present explanation on page 16 arrives after readers have assigned a meaning to that label several times. Mark the exterior-transfer argument on page 8 as supplementary too. The abstract should describe the mathematical result and the proof. Move the `typePrimesN` scores out of it. The test measured agreement with eight items selected by an AI reader for a different theorem. It offers no observation of a mathematician learning this argument. If retained as a methods note, give a versioned link to the key, drafts and grades so that readers can judge what the scores measure.

## 5. Specific comments

1. **Recommended, p. 2, §2.1, and p. 4, theorem statement.** Introduce the diagonal action in words, then give \(G_{ij}=\sum_{p\in S}E_{ij}^{(p)}\). Its adjoint is \(G_{ji}\), so the expectation of \(K\) is already real. The real-part notation matches Lean. Define “zero background” here as the absence of the extra action on the coefficient factor. The rank-one scalar-background example on pp. 7–8 can then be omitted.
2. **Recommended, p. 3, §2.2.** Show directly why the sorted weights give a tableau. The successive differences of \(\nu_\tau\) are the gaps between successive weights minus one, hence nonnegative, and its first value is nonnegative by the choice of \(s\). Thus once \(c<\nu_\tau(i)\) holds, the box exists at every higher label. Cite \(\mathtt{sort\_data}\) in `PKTypeA.lean:207`. This is the part of the construction a reader needs before the proof.
3. **Recommended, p. 8, Proposition 3, paragraph defining \(T\).** Specify that \(A_i^\dagger A_j\) is the *induced Lie algebra action* of the matrix unit on the wedge. On its isometric alternating-tensor realization this is the sum over all slots in the factor. Expanding the product of the two such sums and applying Proposition 1 then gives exactly \(X_{cc'}\). The individual swaps need not preserve the two alternating factors.
4. **Optional, p. 9, Proposition 3, paragraph beginning “Heuristic.”** Delete “the normalized quadratic has leading form” and the surrounding asymptotic discussion. The following exact quadratic is shorter, applies at every target size, and identifies both possible eigenvalues.
5. **Recommended, p. 13, §2.7.** Specify that the compact group \(U(n)\) acts unitarily, so its invariant summands split orthogonally and their complexified Lie algebra action gives the stated scalars. For sharpness, take the first \(h_c\) vectors in the reversed basis in each active wedge and any nonzero alternating tensor on the inactive columns. Equivalently, transfer from a shorter such wedge into a taller one always tries to insert a vector already present. This makes attainment visible in the pair calculation too.
6. **Recommended, p. 13, §2.7, last sentence of the highest-weight paragraph.** “Alpöge's coordinate proof follows” runs directly into five citations to the classical comparison. Attach the citations to the highest-weight argument they support. Begin the coordinate proof with the specialization of \(R\) and \(N\). This removes an unintended suggestion about the provenance of Alpöge's proof.
7. **Recommended, p. 16, §4 and reference [3].** Correct the title to *The ordinary Riemann Hypothesis argument in the supplied Lean source: A mathematical reconstruction for referee checking*, version 2, 22 November 2026. The locator §3.18, pp. 57–59, is appropriate. Use “Rodgers's reconstruction” in the introduction. The description “human-readable” on its title page does not establish a human-only drafting process. [Rodgers, version 2](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24473551)

## 6. Recommendation and venue

**Major revision.** I would reconsider a shorter manuscript for an expository venue such as *The Mathematical Intelligencer*. Readers fluent in linear algebra could follow the entire coordinate proof there, including why an individual swap can have the wrong sign while the sum over a column has the right upper bound. That is a sufficient reason to write the article. A research journal seeking a new representation-theoretic result has less reason to publish it. The revision should give the elementary proof one uninterrupted run, preserve the concordance for readers checking Lean, and correct the scope and attribution of the verification claims.
